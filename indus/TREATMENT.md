# The Mound of the Dead — Mohenjo-daro & Harappa (draft for the style test)

1920×1080 (16:9) · ~45 s · documentary narrator (offline, voice TBD from samples) · original score: harp-like plucked drone, frame drum, bansuri-like flute, non-specific (no recognisable raga)
Style: **Isometric Picture Book** (chosen after the style test). Voice: Kokoro `am_michael` (American, warm).

## Script (trimmed to 45 s)

| id | Line |
|---|---|
| v1 | "Four and a half thousand years ago, while Egypt built its pyramids, a city on the Indus had drains under every street." |
| v2 | "This is Mohenjo-daro. With Harappa, it was the heart of the Indus Valley Civilisation." |
| v3 | "Streets ran in a grid. Bricks were baked to the same proportions. Most homes had their own bathroom." |
| v4 | "Merchants stamped their goods with seals, in a script no one can read, to this day." |
| v5 | "Around 1900 BCE, the rivers shifted, the monsoon weakened, and the cities emptied." |
| v6 | "Four thousand years later, archaeologists dug into a mound called Mohenjo-daro: the Mound of the Dead." |

Facts: mature Harappan c. 2600–1900 BCE (Giza pyramids c. 2600–2500 BCE); covered street drains; standard brick ratio 1:2:4; most houses with bathing areas and wells; seals with an undeciphered script; decline linked to river shifts and a weakening monsoon; Harappa excavated 1921, Mohenjo-daro 1922; "Mohenjo-daro" ≈ "Mound of the Dead" (Sindhi).

## Three candidate structures

1. **One street, then and now** (chosen): the camera travels one continuous street of Mohenjo-daro at its peak (river gate → houses → market), the decline drains colour and sand fills it, and the same street is revealed again as a 1920s excavation. Before/after on the same ground ties the mystery to the place.
2. **A brick's journey** from kiln to wall to drain to market: charming, but the decline and rediscovery sit badly in a brick's story.
3. **A timeline on the river** (sites lighting up along the Indus): clear for geography, weak for daily life, which is the most surprising part.
