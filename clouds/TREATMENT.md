# How a Cloud Is Made

Style: Mid-century Cartoon (scene style: a four-step "how it works" card) · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 24 fps · ~46 s
Voice: calm classroom narrator, offline (Kokoro `bm_george`, British male) · Music: original West-Coast cool jazz
Palette: pastel. Script approved by the client.

## Brief (summed up)

Explain cloud formation in four steps a viewer can repeat back: warm → rise → cool → condense, then the payoff (a cloud) and a tip (rain). Pastel colours.

Facts (textbook values): rising unsaturated air cools ≈ 9.8 °C per km (dry adiabatic lapse rate) → "nearly ten degrees for every kilometre"; vapour condenses on condensation nuclei (dust, sea salt, smoke) → "specks of dust and salt"; a cloud droplet ≈ 10–20 µm → "≈ 0.02 mm"; a cloud holds billions of droplets.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Four numbered steps with a presenter** (the style's own grammar: a held figure, one hand action per step, a medallion iris between steps). *Chosen*: the viewer gets a numbered list they can remember; the medallion iris carries the camera *up* the sky, which is the story of a cloud.
2. **One water molecule's journey** (a droplet character rides from lake to cloud). Charming, but it turns the film into a story and the steps blur; the style says "show how", not narrate.
3. **Do / don't split** (myths vs facts about clouds). Clever, but explains less in 45 s.

Why 1, adapted to vertical: the 9:16 frame is a *column of air*. The film climbs it. Step 1 is at the bottom (the lake), step 4 near the top (the cloud), so every transition is a tilt up through the next medallion.

## Logline and arc

A 1950s classroom presenter shows, in four numbered steps, how sunshine on a lake becomes a cloud, and then how the cloud gives the water back.

- Result first (the cloud) → steps 1–4 climbing the column of air → payoff (four medallions in a row, the cloud complete) → tip (rain, umbrella) → iris out on the cloud.

## Presenter

**Professor Nimbus**: geometric 1950s weather lady. Egg head with the nose in the face line, a butter-yellow bob, lilac A-line dress (her accent: lilac, never used on grounds), white four-finger gloves, a slim pointer stick. Body held; only arms, hands, eyes and mouth animate. Stands at the left third, bottom, so the column above her is free for the science.

## Benchmark

- **1950s classroom science films (grammar)**: one sentence per picture, numbered cards, direct address, calm authority.
- **UPA colour-plane design and atomic-age textiles (grammar)**: flat planes, broken ink, sunbursts, boomerangs.
- Learn: the picture explains, the voice names it; data lands *on* the picture as a tag.
- Don't take: characters, drawings, lettering, music.

## Score and cue map

- Original cool jazz, ♩ = 132 swing, 4/4 (bar ≈ 1.82 s); vibraphone, flute, pizzicato walking bass, brushes, celesta, finger snaps.
- Each step gets **its own vibraphone chord** (1: Fmaj7, 2: Gm7, 3: Am7, 4: B♭maj7), stamped with the medallion; the payoff brings all four back, one per medallion.
- Silence: everything (ambience too) cuts for the beat before droplets form (step 4 action), so the first sound after it, a celesta "plink", is the condensation.
- Ending: flute phrase, rain = descending celesta drops, vibraphone tremolo on the iris out.
