For venues

The tablet behind the bar, but the room pays for it

Every venue has one, and by eleven there is always someone leaning over it. Run a Hijack Session instead: guests request from a list you set, whoever is on keeps control of it, and the argument settles itself because the queue is ordered by what people actually put behind a song.

Your list, start to finish

Load the music you'd have played anyway. Nothing can be requested that isn't already on it, so the room never drags the set somewhere you didn't want it to go.

Someone still runs it

Requests land on a phone behind the bar and a member of staff waves them through. Nothing queues without them — the same control a performer has, in your hands.

Ends the negotiation

No more talking your staff into one more song. If two tables want different things the queue answers it, and it answers with money rather than with whoever is loudest.

A show you can put a name to

Hijack Session, every Thursday. Put it on the board and promote it as something people turn up for, with shout-outs and dedications going up on your screen as part of the deal.

On your stream

Put the queue on the broadcast

The set list is the interesting part: the room chose it, and it changes while people watch. Add one link to OBS, Streamlabs or PRISM and it sits on your stream, updating as requests land — so the people watching can hijack it too.

One link, set once

Paste it in and leave it. It finds each new show on its own, so there is nothing to change between gigs.

Works from a phone

PRISM takes it as a Web widget straight on your handset. No laptop, no capture card, no second person.

Invisible when you're off

It disappears completely when no show is running, so it never leaves a dead box on a stream between shows.

Nothing private on screen

Song, artist and dedications only. No guest names and no amounts — a stream reaches strangers, and it shows them what the room chose, not who paid what.
